01 / THE BIG PICTURE

A global ambition.
A very local impact.

Pax Silica is a US-led framework for cooperation on technology supply chains. Under it, the Philippines is advancing a proposed AI-Native Industrial Acceleration Hub in New Clark City, linking investment, AI, and advanced industry. [2]

Officials describe the local hub as focused on semiconductors and advanced manufacturing. BCDA has rejected the description of it as a giant data-processing center. The exact facilities matter when judging its resource needs. [3]

A proposal, with details still evolving

A November 4, 2026 report said the legal framework was still a draft. This explainer uses reporting available through that date; it does not claim a final agreement has been signed. [4]

FOCUS / AETA COMMUNITIES

Who gets to define progress?

Mongabay reports that Aeta communities claim ancestral land overlapping the planned hub. It also reports concerns about water, energy, and waste. These are reported community concerns, not proof that every feared outcome will occur. [5]

BCDA says the proposed development is outside ancestral domains and promises support for affected communities. The Palace has also said farmers and Indigenous Peoples will not be displaced. Those are official assurances; they do not resolve the competing land claims by themselves. [3] [4]

The question is not only how much land, but whose relationship to that land is recognized.

05 / BEFORE THE GROUNDBREAKING

A better future starts
with better questions.

01 Where exactly are the boundaries?+

Ask for a public, detailed site map alongside land tenure records and community land claims. An area total cannot show which homes, fields, waterways, or cultural places may be affected.

02 How will communities shape the decisions?+

Look for consultation records in accessible languages, clear responses to community concerns, and evidence that Indigenous residents can influence the project before decisions are locked in.

03 What benefits can local people actually access?+

Ask for measurable commitments on local hiring, skills training, supplier access, and livelihood support. Distinguish investment announcements from benefits delivered to households.

04 Who will track environmental performance?+

Look for published assessments, water and energy plans, waste safeguards, independent monitoring, and a process residents can use when something goes wrong.
