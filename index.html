Who gets to shape that transformation?

Pax Silica is a US-led technology supply-chain framework. Under it, the Philippines is advancing a proposed AI-Native Industrial Acceleration Hub in New Clark City. [2]

Officials describe a semiconductor and advanced-manufacturing hub. BCDA rejects the idea of one giant data-processing center. The exact facilities matter when assessing their resource needs. [3]

Proposed. Still taking shape.

As reported November 4, 2026, the legal framework was still a draft. This explainer does not claim a final agreement has been signed. [4]

Whose future

REPORTED CONCERNS

Mongabay reports Aeta ancestral-land claims overlapping the planned hub, alongside concerns over water, energy, and waste. These are reported concerns, not proof that every feared outcome will occur. [5]

OFFICIAL POSITION

BCDA says the project is outside ancestral domains; the Palace says farmers and Indigenous Peoples will not be displaced. These assurances do not by themselves settle competing land claims. [3] [4]

A fair assessment needs community voices, clear boundaries, and public evidence.

Editorial framing. This site does not speak for Aeta communities or assume all Indigenous groups share one view.

★ ★ ★
WHERE EXACTLY ARE THE BOUNDARIES?+

Look for a detailed public site map alongside land tenure records and community land claims. An area total cannot identify affected homes, farms, waterways, or cultural places.

WHO HAS A VOICE IN THE DECISIONS?+

Ask for accessible consultation records, responses to community concerns, and evidence that Indigenous residents can influence plans before decisions are locked in.

WHO WILL TRACK THE PROMISES?+

Seek measurable local hiring commitments, published resource plans, independent monitoring, and a grievance process residents can actually use.

A public-interest checklist proposed by this explainer.
